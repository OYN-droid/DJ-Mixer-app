# Stem Lab

Stem Lab turns compatible recordings into controllable musical parts for remixing, mashups, practice, performance, and production. Separation is treated as a source transformation with known limits, not as a promise of perfectly isolated audio.

## Separation workflow

The artist selects an eligible local or authorized source, chooses an available separation model, reviews time and storage expectations, and starts a job. Progress, cancellation, failure, and retry states must be visible. Completed outputs retain their relationship to the original recording, model version, creation date, and analysis settings.

Stem quality varies with source material and method. DeckForge should allow auditioning, alignment checks, gain adjustment, and replacement. If local processing is unavailable or a source cannot legally or technically be processed, the interface must explain the limitation before offering the action.

## Stem workspace

Stem sets expose musical parts such as vocals, drums, bass, and instruments according to the chosen model. Each part can be soloed, muted, leveled, processed, routed, and synchronized. All stems share an aligned timeline and stable source identity.

Edits are non-destructive. Trims, fades, warping, routing, and effects are project instructions applied to retained source assets. Rendering creates a new artifact and does not erase the editable setup.

## Remix integration

Stem sets can be loaded into supported deck modes, assigned to Pads, arranged in Producer Studio, or used by Smart Mix when capability and performance allow. Mashup tools should propose compatible combinations while disclosing uncertain tempo, key, and structure analysis.

Playback joins the shared master graph and obeys Global Stop, recording, metering, and cleanup rules. Background processing should not degrade live audio; jobs may pause or reduce resource use while performance-critical features are active.

Stem Lab succeeds when separation opens a practical creative path while source rights, processing limits, and project ownership remain clear.
