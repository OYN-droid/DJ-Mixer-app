# Harmony Lab

Harmony Lab is the tonal creation workspace for chords, bass lines, melodies, and harmonic exploration. It should welcome artists who work by ear while offering enough structure for experienced musicians to shape precise results.

## Musical workspace

The primary surfaces are a playable keyboard, chord controls, bass and melody editors, scale and key guidance, and a composer timeline. Notes and chords should be audible immediately, visibly selected, and editable after recording. Computer keyboard input, pointer input, touch, and MIDI should share the same note lifecycle so held notes, sustain, release, and panic behavior remain predictable.

Key and scale choices guide rather than restrict. Out-of-scale notes remain possible and are represented honestly. Chord names, voicings, inversions, extensions, bass relationships, and borrowed harmony should be expressed in musician-friendly language with optional detail.

## Creation workflows

Artists can begin from a key, play freely, select a chord progression, match a reference, or ask for candidates from a prompt. Assisted results should be previewable and should expose the notes they add. Applying a progression must create ordinary editable project material, not a locked AI object.

Match tools may consider a deck track, Beat Forge pattern, sample, stem, or existing project section. They should distinguish verified key information from analysis and allow the artist to correct the tonal center.

## Recording and integration

Harmony Lab records notes, timing, duration, velocity, sustain, and relevant automation into clips or arrangement regions. Quantization and humanization are reversible operations. Bass, chord, and melody parts remain separately editable even when generated together.

Playback uses the shared audio architecture and follows project tempo where appropriate. Harmony Lab can send material to Pads, Producer Studio, or arrangement workflows without rendering away editability. It should also offer a reliable stop or panic action for stuck notes.

The goal is to turn harmonic curiosity into playable material while keeping theory supportive, sound immediate, and every note owned by the artist.
