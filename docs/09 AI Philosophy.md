# AI Philosophy

DeckForge uses artificial intelligence to widen creative possibility, reduce repetitive work, and help artists understand their options. It does not replace taste, timing, selection, or performance. The artist remains the author, and every AI-assisted action must preserve that relationship.

## The AI contract

AI may suggest, explain, compare, organize, and generate candidates. It must not silently change playback, overwrite work, or present uncertain analysis as fact. A trustworthy interaction follows a visible sequence:

1. Understand the relevant project context.
2. Present a recommendation, plan, or preview.
3. Explain the important reasoning and tradeoffs.
4. Wait for explicit application when the result changes the project.
5. Preserve a direct path to undo, revise, or reject the result.

Manual controls are always authoritative. If an artist adjusts a deck, pattern, chord, pad, stem, or arrangement after an AI suggestion, the new manual state becomes the source of truth.

## Useful intelligence

AI should speak in musical terms and make concrete proposals. A useful response might identify a safer transition because of tempo distance, propose a shorter phrase length, find tracks that support a narrative turn, or offer several chord voicings with different emotional weight. Generic encouragement and unexplained scores do not help an artist make a decision.

Recommendations should disclose the context they used, the confidence of inferred information, and any capability limits. Verified metadata, locally analyzed attributes, user-entered facts, and model inferences are distinct kinds of information and should remain distinguishable.

## Creative control and privacy

AI features are optional. Core playback and creation workflows must remain usable without them. When a feature requires network access, account authorization, or sending project information to an external service, DeckForge should explain that boundary before the action begins.

Prompts, listening history, library metadata, and unfinished music are sensitive creative material. DeckForge should collect only what a feature needs, retain it according to a clear policy, and provide meaningful controls over deletion and reuse.

The standard is simple: intelligence should make the artist feel more capable, never less in control.
