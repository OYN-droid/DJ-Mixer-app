# User Experience

DeckForge supports three overlapping user profiles. A person may begin in one profile and grow into another without changing products or rebuilding projects.

## Beginner DJ

The beginner wants to hear a good result quickly and understand what to do next. DeckForge should provide welcoming empty states, safe import paths, obvious deck transport, a clear crossfader, starter pad and beat options, and an unmistakable Stop All Audio control.

The beginner experience should:

- Avoid requiring knowledge of signal flow before playback
- Explain tempo, phrasing, key, and transitions in practical language
- Offer Smart Mix as help, not as the only route
- Keep mistakes recoverable
- Celebrate progress without grading the user

## Working DJ

The working DJ values speed, reliability, preparation, and manual authority. The library must support fast search and organization. Decks must respond predictably. Performance controls must remain available during automation. Recording and export should preserve the set.

The working DJ experience should:

- Make playback ownership and transition state visible
- Support quick loading, cueing, looping, and correction
- Allow Smart Mix plans to be previewed and overridden
- Keep DITC, pads, stems, and arrangement close to the live workflow
- Treat stopping, recovery, and state persistence as performance-critical

## Producer

The producer turns musical ideas into patterns, layers, arrangements, and reusable project assets. Beat Forge, Harmony Lab, Pads, Stem Lab, and Producer Studio should share timing, project identity, and arrangement destinations.

The producer experience should:

- Preserve one canonical model for each pattern or performance
- Support manual creation before generation
- Make AI suggestions specific to the current project
- Offer preview, apply, explanation, and undo
- Allow ideas to move between workspaces without losing timing or metadata

## Shared expectations

All users deserve accessible controls, readable status, low-latency feedback, honest capability boundaries, and confidence that their work will not disappear. Simple and Advanced modes change depth, not product quality.
